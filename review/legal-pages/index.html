Worth a look before launch

Things the code does today that the pages now say plainly. A lawyer will likely ask about them.

  • Arena bots look like people. In the snake, cell, Paper and maze games they have ordinary names, no bot label, and the lobby counts them as players. In paid rooms they carry the room's buy-in from the Bot bank, and players win or lose real money against them. The Terms say this in those words.
  • Paid duel bots (branch owner/bots). The Terms already describe them, so they stay true whether that branch merges or not. Before it merges: that branch also edits public/terms.html (keep the new Computer players section); the Swim bot is built to be "as good as it can get"; the "Bot opponent waiting" consent is a note in the browser that the server trusts; and server/gamePageData.js and two Updates posts still say paid duels are always against a real person.
  • No age check, no region block, no limits or self-exclusion. The pages say so instead of promising them.
  • PostHog session recording is on for every visitor, with no opt-out (question 9).
  • Anyone can look up any wallet's profile, buy-ins included, through /api/my-profile?wallet= with no sign-in. The Privacy page says so. If you would rather it did not, that route needs a sign-in check, and then that line can go.
  • Nothing is ever deleted from the database, and there is no delete tool. Accounts from the old Google sign-in may still hold an email address.
  • Sample players fill the leaderboards and the recent wins list on main (SAMPLE_BOARD and SAMPLE_WINS on). The Terms say they are all marked "Sample".
  • An overpaid buy-in keeps the extra. The site asks for the exact amount, so this should be rare.
